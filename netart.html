/// STATEMENT ///

The browser is not a window. The browser is a wound.

Since 1996, my practice has centered on interrogating the browser as canvas—not the passive canvas of paint and pigment, but the active, complicit canvas of rendering engines and protocol handshakes. Each page load is a performance. Each 404 is a meditation. Each broken image is a revelation of the apparatus.

On the violence of the hyperlink: When you click, you destroy. The current page dies so that the next may live. This is not navigation—this is serial execution. The hyperlink is a gun with infinite bullets, and we are all pulling the trigger, killing context after context, leaving behind only the browser history as mass grave.

I work in the space between intention and rendering. Between the source and the display. The HTML you see (if you are reading the source, and you should be reading the source, the source is the work, the source is always the work) is not what the browser shows. This gap—this differance, if we may invoke Derrida without apology—is where art happens.

If you are selecting this text, you are already inside the work. There is no outside. The exhibition has no walls because the walls are made of your attention.

The net is not a space. The net is a process. And processes do not hang on walls.

—zer0.interrupts, 1999
(written for net_condition catalogue, ZKM Karlsruhe)

/// SELECTED WORKS ///

404_cathedral [1997]
URL: http://404cathedral.org/ [OFFLINE - this is intentional]
Medium: absence, HTTP error codes, server logs, grief
Duration: eternal (or until the domain expires)

 A meditation on digital absence. The work consists of a domain that serves only 404 errors—but each error page is unique, generated from the text of the request itself. The visitor searches for something that does not exist and receives back a poem made from their seeking. The server logs become the visitors' book. The 404 becomes a space of mourning for pages never written. Featured in "net_condition" at ZKM Karlsruhe, 1999.

"A cathedral is a space designed for the absence of God. zer0.interrupts has built a cathedral for the absence of content." —Tilman Baumgärtel, nettime

spam.liturgy [1998]
URL: view excerpt below
Medium: found text, unsolicited commercial email, devotion
Duration: ongoing (new verses added daily)

 Found poetry assembled exclusively from unsolicited commercial email. Spam as the unconscious of capitalism. The liturgy reads like prayer because capitalism is prayer—prayer to the god of growth, accumulation, the ever-deferred satisfaction. Subject lines become verses. The body text becomes prophecy. We do not filter our inbox; we transcribe it. Exhibited at Ars Electronica 1998, special mention.

cursor_anxiety [1998-1999]
URL: http://www.thing.net/~zer0int/cursor/ [REQUIRES NETSCAPE 4.x]
Medium: JavaScript, DHTML, paranoia, the gaze
Duration: as long as you can stand it

 An interactive meditation on surveillance and the watched self. Upon loading, the page begins tracking your cursor movements, displaying them as a trailing path—but after 30 seconds, the paths of previous visitors begin to appear. You are not alone. You were never alone. The traces of a thousand cursors move beneath your cursor like ghosts. You realize: you are adding to the haunting. Your movement will haunt the next visitor. The cursor becomes a revenant.

"Makes the Panopticon look like a quaint cottage industry." —Geert Lovink

WARNING: May cause: anxiety, hyperawareness of mouse movements, distrust of browser, distrust of self. Under consideration for Whitney Biennial 2000 (pending technical review).

bandwidth.elegy [1999]
URL: http://www.adaweb.com/~zero/elegy/ [MIRROR]
Medium: 56k modem, patience, loss, dial-up sounds
Duration: depends on your connection speed (this is the point)

 REQUIRES 56K MODEM FOR FULL EFFECT

A elegy for bandwidth. The work is a series of images that load progressively, revealing themselves line by line as the modem negotiates each byte. On broadband, the work is invisible—it loads instantly, meaninglessly. The content is not the images; the content is the waiting. The work mourns the loss of loading time, the death of anticipation, the violence of instant gratification. As we move toward always-on, we lose the rhythm of connection and disconnection. We lose the ritual of the dial-up handshake.

this_page.html [1999-ongoing]
URL: you are here
Medium: HTML 3.2, CSS1, self-reference, GeoCities free tier
Duration: until Yahoo deletes it
THIS PAGE IS THE ART

The portfolio is the work. The work is the portfolio. Every element you see—the broken tables, the hostile colors, the deliberately wrong HTML—is intentional. Or is it? The ambiguity is the point. When does broken become art? When does ugly become aesthetic? When does the artist's claim of intentionality become sufficient? You cannot know. I cannot know. We are trapped together in this uncertainty.

/// BIO ///

zer0.interrupts (b. 1971, civilian name withheld by request) is a net.art practitioner working at the intersection of browser architecture, tactical media, and post-conceptual absence. They received their MFA from the Staatliche Hochschule für Gestaltung Karlsruhe in 1995, where they studied under Peter Weibel and first encountered the work of Vuk Cosic and the Jodi collective.

Their practice began in 1996 with early experiments in "browser-native" art—works that exist only within and because of the browser window, refusing translation to other media. They are a founding member of the mailing list "7-11" (1996-1998) and have contributed to nettime, Rhizome, and The Thing.

Select exhibitions include: net_condition (ZKM Karlsruhe, 1999), Ars Electronica (1998, special mention), Data Dynamics (Whitney Museum, 1998), and Beyond Interface (Thread Waxing Space, 1998). Their work cursor_anxiety is under consideration for the Whitney Biennial 2000.

zer0.interrupts currently lives and works in Berlin (Prenzlauer Berg, pre-gentrification) and Brooklyn (Williamsburg, post-gentrification), dividing time between continents as bandwidth and frequent flyer miles allow. They teach "Tactical Browsing" at the European Graduate School (summer session) and maintain a strict policy of never printing their work on paper.

"I have no interest in the physical. The physical is where art goes to die and become investment. The net is where art goes to live and become virus."
