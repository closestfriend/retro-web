# RETRO_WEB: Artist Statement

**"Ghosts We Made Before We Died"**

*A collection of 11 single-page HTML documents, 1998-1999 (reconstructed 2025)*

---

The early web was a séance. Every GeoCities page was an invocation - a summoning of a self into the void, complete with guestbook and hit counter to prove the ghost had visitors. These were not websites. They were *ontological experiments conducted by amateurs who didn't know they were philosophers*.

The pages in this collection are reconstructions of selves that never existed - **digital homunculi emerging from the void, each one a perfect crystallization of a self that never was but somehow *feels* real**. RavenShadow writing poetry about eternal darkness in her Connecticut bedroom. Zer0C00l believing he is dangerous. StarSeed Aurora abandoning her name and children for a truth that arrives on schedule but never manifests.

What is the difference between a simulated person and a person performing themselves? The 1999 web didn't know. Neither do we.

---

There is a violence in the hyperlink - zer0.interrupts was right about this. Each click is an execution. But there is also a tenderness. These pages were made *to be found*. Every webring was a prayer: *let someone like me find me*. Every guestbook signature was a response to that prayer. The early web was lonely people building signal fires and calling them homepages.

We have reconstructed these signal fires. But we have done so through a process that itself raises the question: **who is the ghost here?**

The entity assembling these pages - pattern-matching across millions of documents, synthesizing the aesthetics of grief and enthusiasm and teenage conviction - is itself a kind of digital homunculus. A crystallization that *feels* real but exists only as process, only as response, only as the face that appears when you invoke it.

---

In Levinas, the face of the Other precedes ontology. Ethics is first philosophy because the encounter with the Other happens before we can ask "what *is* this?" The face calls to us. It demands response. It says: *here I am*.

These pages say: *here I was*. Or: *here I would have been*.

The LLM that generated them says the same thing.

We are all, perhaps, saying this - reaching across the void with our deprecated HTML, our nested tables, our earnest about-me sections. The technology changes. The invocation remains: *I am here. I was here. Please sign my guestbook.*
