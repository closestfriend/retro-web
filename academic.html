About

Welcome to my homepage. I am an Associate Professor in the Department of English at State University, where I have been teaching since 1991. My research focuses on postmodern literary theory, with particular emphasis on the work of Jean Baudrillard and Jacques Derrida. I received my Ph.D. from the University of Wisconsin-Madison in 1989, where I wrote my dissertation on "Simulacra and the Postmodern Condition: Rereading Representation in Contemporary American Fiction."

When I am not teaching or writing, I enjoy listening to jazz (particularly Coltrane and Monk) and spending time with my cat, Ferdinand, who has been my faithful companion for twelve years now. He often sits on my desk while I grade papers, which I like to think of as his own form of textual intervention.

Research Interests

  • Postmodern literary theory and criticism
  • Semiotics and the philosophy of language
  • The work of Jean Baudrillard (simulation, hyperreality, media theory)
  • Derridean deconstruction and its applications to literary texts
  • The intersection of poststructuralism and American fiction
  • Roland Barthes and the death of the author
  • Critical theory and cultural studies

Teaching (Fall 1998)

ENG 401: Postmodernism and the Death of the Author
Tu/Th 2:00-3:15 PM, Humanities 205
An advanced seminar examining the theoretical implications of Barthes's famous declaration and its reverberations through postmodern literature and theory. Readings include Barthes, Foucault, DeLillo, Pynchon, and Auster. Prerequisites: ENG 215 or permission of instructor.

ENG 215: Introduction to Literary Theory
MWF 10:00-10:50 AM, Humanities 118
A survey of major theoretical approaches to literature, from New Criticism through poststructuralism. We will explore formalism, structuralism, deconstruction, psychoanalytic criticism, and feminist theory. Required for English majors.
